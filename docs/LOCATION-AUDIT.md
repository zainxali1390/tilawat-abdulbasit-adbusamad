## How each recording was checked

1. **Its own labels**: the file name, and the ID3 title / album / comment tags and upload description on archive.org (`research/meta.json`, every file, read for this audit). Labels saying "A or B" were not taken as an answer.
2. **The same audio elsewhere** in the library: all recordings were compared by sound (landmark fingerprints, a straight time line fitted over the whole length; `tools/library/same_audio_library.py`). Where an exact copy is labelled at a venue, that venue was taken, **only if** every copy and its own label agree on the country and place. If they disagree, nothing was changed.
3. **What is said in the recording**: the first 60 s and last 45 s of every recording were transcribed with an Arabic speech model (`research/announcements.json`), looking for an announcer or the Sheikh naming a mosque, city, station or occasion. Every hit was read by hand and is quoted in the evidence (the recogniser misspells words; the quotes are corrected where the meaning is clear).
4. **Other uploads of the same audio on archive.org**: every other Abdul Basit upload on archive.org whose label names a place (18728 files) was matched by sound against the library (`research/labelled-uploads-check.json`); a match counts only when at least two 25-second windows agree at the same offset. Their labels were then read for a place.

**Rules for "confirmed"**: every source that names a place must agree. A recording already filed under a country that its own label does not name is moved to another country only on two independent kinds of evidence (e.g. its announcement and labelled copies). "Al-Aqsa" labels on other uploads are not taken alone, because several uploaders attribute Kuwait and UAE recordings to Al-Aqsa (their own notes say so). A label that says "A or B" is not an answer. Where a radio recording is identified, the place is the station that broadcast it (as the library does for all radio recordings), and it stays in the Radio collection.
5. **Web search** (Arabic and English) about the tours with the most unplaced recordings (South Africa 1966, Kuwait 1964). It confirmed the tours but named no venue for any particular recording, so it changed nothing.

Not used as evidence: recording quality, the surahs recited (the same passage was recited at many events), the collection a file was uploaded in (one exception: an upload whose own title names the station, I12 "إذاعيات مصر"), or where a copy was later broadcast.
